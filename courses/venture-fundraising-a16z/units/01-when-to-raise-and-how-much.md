terms:
  - runway
  - burn rate
  - milestone
  - dilution
  - product/market fit
  - SAFE
  - bridge round
points:
  - Raise to reach the milestones the next round’s investors will want to see, with a cushion
  - Raise when you have runway to negotiate, milestones that justify the price, and are ready for diligence
  - Starting a raise with less than about 12 months of cash weakens your position
  - In a hard market, a bigger round at more dilution can beat a better price with a shorter runway
  - Judge your stage by what you have proven (team, product fit, repeatable sales, unit economics), not by round names
  - Spending lean or fast is a tactic; the goals are winning the market and not running out of cash
  - Debt can complement equity for predictable needs; it does not replace equity
activities:
  - Tell the teacher your next three milestones; together, turn them into a round size and a timeline
  - "Role play: you are the founder and the teacher is a Series B investor a year from now. What must be true?"
  - "Argue both sides: raise a smaller round now, or wait three months for better numbers?"
pitfalls:
  - Raising without knowing how much is needed or what for
  - Waiting until cash is short, then negotiating from weakness
  - Treating round names (seed, Series A) as fixed thresholds
  - 'Assuming a rule like "18–24 months of runway" comes from a16z: no a16z source states it'
