points:
  - Run an organized process with enough investors to create competition, focused on those likely to close
  - "Leverage moves to the investor once a term sheet is signed: close it, do not let it drift"
  - Start with investors who fit your stage; it is hard to go back to earlier-stage ones later
  - A 2025 a16z piece argues for a structured process over waiting quietly to be preempted
  - Artificial deadlines and pretending not to raise put serious investors off
  - Ask existing investors early whether they will take their pro rata; a no can read as a bad sign
  - Prepare the data room before you launch the process
activities:
  - Build your investor list together and decide the order to meet them in
  - "Role play the end of a first meeting: make the ask and handle the partner’s reply"
  - The teacher proposes a tactic; you say whether it helps or backfires and why
pitfalls:
  - Taking meetings while claiming not to raise
  - Skipping junior partners, who often hold the real expertise
  - Letting a signed term sheet drift or reopen
  - No backup plan if the round does not close
